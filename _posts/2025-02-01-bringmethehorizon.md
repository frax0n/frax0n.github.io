## Bring Me The Horizon: My Introduction to Metalcore  

**Bring Me The Horizon** was the first band that truly got me into the metalcore genre. This is where I really started to appreciate distorted vocals. The screaming is actually much more difficult than many people believe.  

My obsession with this band started in **2022**—something pretty evident from the huge tattoo I have dedicated to them.  
 
![Sempiternal](/assets/img/BringMeTheHorizon/tattoo.jpg)  

It's a stylized take on their logo, a **hexagram**, which represents the philosophy of **Thelema**:  

> *"Do what thou wilt shall be the whole of the Law."*  

This was also when I started exploring **electric guitars**, as I fell in love with distorted guitar riffs. Songs like *Shadow Moses* and *Ludens* are amazing, especially with their **drop tunings**.  

## Here are some of my favourite albums by them:-

#### A Guide for Those Wanting to Get into Metal  

## Sempiternal  

![Sempiternal](/assets/img/BringMeTheHorizon/Sempiternal.jpg)  

This is the album that propelled **Bring Me The Horizon** into the mainstream. I believe this album, in particular, gave them a huge audience, and almost everyone has heard *Can You Feel My Heart*—thanks to the **Chad music meme**. The meme community has fully embraced this song.  

In my opinion, the best song on this album is either **Shadow Moses** or **Sleepwalking**.  

- *Shadow Moses* was the song that made me appreciate **faster drums** paired with **screaming vocals**. The line *"This is Sempiternal!"* is simply epic. Interestingly, *Shadow Moses* is also a tribute to the **Metal Gear Solid** series, referencing the first game where you play as **Solid Snake** against his twin, **Liquid Snake**.  
- *Sleepwalking* has a **slower tempo** and, in my opinion, is even more impactful with its great lyrics.  
- *House of Wolves* feels like a mix of *Shadow Moses* and *Sleepwalking*.  

The rest of the album is also great but leans more towards the **metalcore community**, so it might not suit everyone's taste.  

---

## That's the Spirit  

![That's the Spirit](/assets/img/BringMeTheHorizon/ThatsTheSpirit.jpg)  

This album capitalized on the success of *Sempiternal* and aimed to bring in **new fans**. It’s not a **metalcore album** but rather **alternative rock**.  

- *Follow You* is a great example of a song meant to draw people in. It’s a **pop song**, which isn’t typically their style, but they proved they can **excel at clean vocals and pop music** if they really wanted to. Essentially, they were screaming, *"My boy Ollie CAN SING!"*  
- Songs like *True Friends, Throne, Avalanche,* and *Drown* belong in a similar category—Ollie showcases his **impactful vocals** with strong choruses and subtle verses. These songs, in my opinion, were aimed at fans of **Linkin Park**.  
- *Doomed* and *Oh No* are **electronic explorations** by the band, something they pushed further in their **2019 album Amo**.  

## Amo

![That's the Spirit](/assets/img/BringMeTheHorizon/Amo.jpg)  

This feels like a compilation of the experiments the band was doing to find their new, distinguishing musical signature. This album leans heavily on electronics and synthesizers for the music.  

A lot of people were upset because they had strayed further from their Metalcore roots. The band acknowledges and simultaneously mocks these sentiments in the song *Heavy Metal*. I emphasize *acknowledges* because they do return to their roots—but better than before.  

- **In the Dark**, **Nihilist Blues**, **Medicine**, and **I Don’t Know What to Say** are the pop songs in the album. Very catchy and easy to get into.  
- **Mantra** and **Wonderful Life** spiritually belong in *That’s the Spirit*, in my opinion.  
- **Sugar Honey Ice & Tea**, **I Apologise If You Feel Something**, **Why You Gotta Kick Me Down?**, and **Ouch** are electronic synth-driven tracks. This is peak experimentation, and they are super fun to listen to.
